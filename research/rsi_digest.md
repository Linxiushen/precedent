## Part 1 — What 'RSI' means in September 2026 (six scout narratives)

### Angle: 
By September 2026 "RSI" is used at three distinct altitudes, and the benchmarks split along the same lines.

The top altitude is model-level RSI: can an agent improve the process that produces models? RSI-Exam (aiming-lab, 88 executable research tasks, 6 domains, sealed-split replay) and AI4AI-Bench (10 frozen training repos, rewrite the learning algorithm, hidden evaluator rerun from scratch) are the reference points, with PostTrainBench (556 stars) as the post-training analogue. The numbers are sober. On RSI-Exam the best system, Opus 5, scores 0.464 on a scale where 0.60 is a frontier-calibrated human reference, and every model loses 11.7-24.7% moving from the visible to the sealed split. AI4AI-Bench's mean is 0.166 (best 0.250): agents close under a fifth of the baseline-to-optimum gap, and only 8% of submissions even attempt to change the learning mechanism unless reasoning effort is cranked up (then 64%). The Princeton study covered by MIT Technology Review (Aug 18) reached the same conclusion qualitatively: given six days and $3k, Opus 4.8 executed engineering but produced papers without novel contributions. Jack Clark called it a bearish signal for short RSI timelines.

The middle altitude, where most "self-evolving agent" papers live, is harness-level evolution: the model is frozen and the loop edits prompts, skills, memories, tools and control flow. This is where the summer's loudest positive claim sits. Weco's AIDE² (July 14) ran an outer loop for 100 iterations over eight days, kept 7 survivors, and reported the evolved agent beating a two-year hand-tuned harness on held-out MLE-Bench Lite, ALE-Bench Lite, WeatherBench 2 and KernelBench. Weco itself concedes there was no "ignition" (the discovered agent did not improve the outer loop) and the report is self-published.

The critique wave that followed is the most important thing a framework designer should absorb, because it is convergent across independent groups:

1. Attribution. AI2/UW ("Rethinking the Evaluation of Harness Evolution", Terminal-Bench 2.1, GPT-5.4 and Opus 4.6) shows harness evolution does not consistently beat simple test-time scaling under matched feedback and inference budgets, and that searching and reporting on the same public benchmark inflates gains. Gideoni/Risi/Gal (ICLR 2026) showed trivial baselines match sophisticated code-evolution pipelines; search space and prompt knowledge set the ceiling. "Worth Their Tokens" finds a budget-matched vanilla actor matches or beats online skill and memory modules on WebArena/WorkArena.

2. Compounding and retention. RELAI's "Do Agent Optimizers Compound?" finds all three optimizers (GEPA, Meta Harness, VCL) win in the one-shot setting, but GEPA's evolved agent transfers below the unoptimized baseline when new tasks arrive and Meta Harness cannot improve again. SEAGym (Tsinghua) shows frequent updates often fail to improve held-out performance and useful snapshots collapse later. EvoHarnessBench (Salesforce, Sept 3) names "harness-induced forgetting": simply adding tools/skills degrades previously solved tasks, and retention and adaptation pull in opposite directions. Adaptive Auto-Harness documents the peak-then-decline curve on open-ended streams.

3. Who benefits. Lin et al. separate harness-updating from harness-benefit: even Qwen3.5-9B produces updates as useful as Opus 4.6, but benefit is non-monotonic and peaks at mid-tier models. HarnessDev (Sept 1) finds evolution gains are unstable, transfer only partially to held-out tasks, and depend strongly on the executing model.

4. Mechanism. EvoTrace/EvoReplay shows most score gains come from a few edit types and ~30% of added lines are byte-identical re-introductions of deleted code. Demystifying Agent Skills shows skills act as procedural anchors (65.7%) not knowledge (4.5%) and retrieval precision collapses from 29.6% to 3.3% as the pool grows to 100, so growing skill libraries self-sabotage. Counterfactual Trace Auditing finds +0.3 pp aggregate effect while behavior changes substantially.

5. Misevolution. SkillMisevo-Bench: all 21 evolved configurations author unsafe artifacts, 15 cause fresh-session harm, because evolution optimizes outcomes not procedure safety. MemEvoBench shows the same for memory under noisy or adversarial inputs.

Who drives this: Chinese university labs (Tsinghua, Fudan, RUC, UCSC/aiming-lab), AI2/UW, Salesforce, Scale (HarnessOpt-Bench), PSU/Amazon, RELAI and Weco on the vendor side, with Karpathy's autoresearch (95k stars) as the cultural substrate and the ICLR 2026 RSI workshop (Schmidhuber et al.) as the venue.

What the community says is unsolved: (a) attribution of gains to harness design vs extra search or compute, requiring matched-budget baselines by default; (b) generalization, requiring sealed splits, OOD views and rerun-from-scratch; (c) compounding, requiring phase-2 evaluation, retention tests and versioned snapshots with replay; (d) safety of evolved artifacts; (e) cost, since token use diverges up to 31x at equal success (SEA-Eval); (f) non-stationary evaluators and reward hacking (Red Queen Gödel Machine); (g) statistically valid acceptance gates for keep/discard decisions (anytime-valid certificates, PACE). The survey of 1,250 papers (2607.07663) frames today's practice as "bounded self-refinement," not open-ended RSI, and calls governance-grade measurement of self-improvement the emptiest area. A harness that ships in 2026 should treat these seven as its evaluation spec.

### Angle: 
In September 2026 "RSI" has stopped meaning a singularity thought-experiment and become a structural claim about a loop. The lists converge on one operational definition: self-refinement improves the current output; persistent self-improvement changes weights, memory, skills, prompts, harness or code so the next round inherits it; recursive self-improvement is when the mechanism that produces improvements is itself the object of improvement. Tencent Hunyuan/ZJU's 549-paper survey (Awesome-Reliable-Self-Evolving-Agents, 197 stars) makes this precise: L0 output, L1 model, L2 scaffold, L3 improver, L4 criterion, and RSI "begins at L3." D2I's survey says the same with an L1-L5 grading where L4 "Bounded RSI" requires rewriting one's own improvement mechanism and L5 adds domain generality. leezythu's Optimization Ladder (L0 prompts to L5 harness+weights) is the version framework designers use most, because it names what is mutated at each rung.

Who drives it. Three overlapping communities: (1) frontier labs, where OpenAI has a public March-2028 target for automated AI researchers, Anthropic reports Claude picking better research paths than humans 64% of the time, and both ship models (GPT-6 Astra, Claude Fable/Mythos 5.1) scored on Terminal-Bench-Science and RSI-Exam; (2) academic/industrial groups producing roughly 80 RSI preprints in Jul-Aug alone: ByteDance Seed (HarnessDev), Salesforce (EvoHarnessBench, Fragility), Stanford IRIS (Meta-Harness, WHALE), KAIST (J-Zero), Minnesota NLP (Meta^n), RUC (SkillAdam), UW/AI2 (Rethinking); (3) list maintainers. Attention-wise, the big stars still sit on older "self-evolving agents" (ANative-Lab 2.5k) and "harness engineering" (ai-boost 4.2k) vocabularies; every repo literally named awesome-rsi was created between Aug 8 and Sep 13 2026 (15+ of them), and the best are lobehub (164), KaiWU5 AI4AI (166) and wkqdzkd (197). The word "RSI" is a five-week-old brand on top of a two-year-old field.

Hot claims. MetaRSI/RSI2 (Sep 9) composes Data-, Harness- and Model-RSI operators under a meta-agent that rewrites their scheduling, reporting +10.9 on Qwen3.5-35B and +7.3 for frontier models from harness edits alone. JIT-Agent (466 stars) trains a 27B model to write harnesses on the fly and lifts DeepSeek-V4-Flash past GPT-5.6. HarnessDev shows model-built harnesses match human ones on writing and ML tasks. Meta^n claims emergent meta-depth and the only non-zero ARC-AGI-2 among self-improvers. RSI-Exam's sealed-replay leaderboard puts GPT-6-astra at 0.51 of the frontier-anchored scale. The engineering consensus in the lists and in commentary is "model-harness co-evolution": freeze weights, evolve skills/memory/scaffold because it is cheap, auditable and reversible, then optionally fold gains into weights (WHALE, NeoHorse-1, SafeEvolve).

The debate. A skeptical bloc now has its own section in awesome-harness-evolution. "Rethinking the Evaluation of Harness Evolution" shows that under matched feedback and inference budgets on Terminal-Bench 2.1, harness evolution does not beat simple test-time scaling and generalizes poorly. "Harness Updating Is Not Harness Benefit" finds update-quality flat across model tiers, benefit non-monotonic, and many automated updates silently regress without a benefit gate. "Do Agent Optimizers Compound?" finds most gains one-shot. "Phantom Gains" reproduces apparent improvement with frozen controls; "Fragility" shows seed and task-order sensitivity; EvoHarnessBench documents harness-induced forgetting; the "Budget-Splitting Trap" paper argues the standard loop misallocates evaluation compute. Public skeptics (Narayanan, Marcus) frame current results as faster engineering, not research; the consensus phrase is "bounded self-refinement, real and compounding but anchored to human direction."

What the community says is unsolved, and what it implies for a verification framework. Every taxonomy insists that the evidence source and acceptance gate live outside the loop's writable surface (wkqdzkd's Figure 1, leezythu's Challenges, MetaRSI's authority boundaries, HackProbe's secret comparison core). pinkbubblebubble's checklist is the emerging reporting standard: multi-generation results including regressions and variance, a held-out verifier the system cannot rewrite, ablations of self-modification vs search vs memory, generalization to unselected tasks, full compute/trajectory/failed-attempt logs, and explicit isolation, permissions, rollback and human interventions. Open problems named repeatedly: weak or fuzzy evaluators and lack of "research taste"; criterion drift when the judge itself evolves (J-Zero, Who Grades the Grader); tampering and provenance (Auditing Harness Tampering, EvoUndo recoverability); skill misevolution and malicious-skill persistence; diversity collapse; longitudinal evaluation under changing workloads; and the L4-to-L5 jump from bounded to general RSI. The practical takeaway for designers: measure benefit, not update count; promote on held-out replay; keep a frozen-control null; treat harness edits as versioned, reversible, provenance-tagged artifacts; and expect reviewers to ask for matched-budget test-time-scaling baselines.

### Angle: 
By September 2026 "recursive self-improvement" has split into two meanings, and most of the heat comes from people talking past each other. The original Yudkowsky sense - an instance rewriting the cognition it runs on - survives mainly in safety writing (Lermen). The dominant lab sense, set by Anthropic Institute's June 4 report "When AI builds itself" (Clark/Favaro) and OpenAI's Sept 6 "An Alien Mind" (Pachocki), is "AI doing a growing share of the R&D that produces its successor." Anthropic's numbers (>80% of merged production code by Claude, 8x code per engineer-quarter, ~52x on code-optimization tasks) and OpenAI's "research intern" milestone (3.1 agent-workdays per human-workday; full automated researcher targeted for March 2028) are the claims everyone argues about.

The academic community, via the ICLR 2026 RSI workshop (Schmidhuber/Zhuge, 110 papers) and the 1,250-paper survey arXiv 2607.07663, has converged on a vocabulary that framework designers should adopt. The survey's split is bounded self-refinement (improve against a fixed external evaluator; convergent, evaluable) versus open-ended RSI (the loop also modifies its own evaluator or improvement machinery; divergent in principle). Almost everything demonstrated is bounded and human-on-the-loop. Its four-rung verification hierarchy - formal verifiers, execution feedback, learned judges, intrinsic signals - is the field's working law: "demonstrated self-improvement strength tracks this hierarchy," and "the difference between a loop that improves and a loop that circles is one rung of external verification." MetaRSI (2609.06396) quantifies the same point: 69% of RSI systems close their loop only on machine-checkable targets. The Sept 10 survey "The Last AI Built by Humans" adds a five-level autonomy ladder (L1 execute a human procedure to L5 revise the improvement mechanism itself) and insists that a higher score does not show the improver was "revised, retained, and reused."

Who drives the term: Anthropic and OpenAI (as a policy and safety framing), Sakana's RSI Lab and Jeff Clune's group (evolutionary and self-referential agents: DGM, Hyperagents), Karpathy (autoresearch, 95k stars, which made propose-train-evaluate-keep a folk pattern), Lilian Weng (whose July 4 post is the canonical harness-first text), the forecasting layer (Forethought's software-intelligence-explosion model, AI Futures Project's quarterly timeline updates, Epoch's counter-analyses), and METR as the measurement referee. A large share of open infrastructure is Chinese: Shanghai AI Lab's Self-Harness, FrontisAI's OpenRSI, CosmosMind's MetaRSI, aiming-lab's RSI-Exam, reef.

Hot debates. (1) Is it happening? Yes for bounded loops with executable feedback; no for direction-setting. METR's expenditure horizon found frontier agents buy 1-1.5% NanoGPT speedups for $10K against ~$250K of human effort - "minimal effect" on frontier progress - and its May Frontier Risk Report recorded that Anthropic had not seen a 2x pace increase as of April. Kapoor's Princeton study had Opus 4.8's NeurIPS-style papers rejected by their own authors. Anthropic itself concedes research taste and direction-setting remain human. (2) Bounded or unbounded? Forethought says r>1 is likely (60% odds of compressing 3+ years into one); Epoch argues compute and cognitive labor may be complements; Lambert's "lossy self-improvement" predicts linear progress from friction and supervisory bottlenecks; Ord shows generation-time floors matter more than per-iteration gain; Burtsev's criticality model warns supercriticality can precede any visible acceleration. (3) Harness-first versus weights-first: Weng, RHI (Sakana/Berkeley), Self-Harness and auto-harness argue the harness is cheap, inspectable, portable and already editable by the agent, so RSI starts there; SIA, NeoHorse and OpenRSI show harness-plus-weights beats harness alone; MetaRSI formalizes admissible orderings (Harness→Model is forbidden because the data predates the capability change) and the consensus is co-evolution with harness traces as training data.

What the community says is unsolved, in rough priority: verification for non-verifiable work (taste, novelty, research direction) - "the trillion-dollar question"; whether a self-trained verifier escapes circularity or merely relocates it; evaluation under repeated evaluator access (cherry-picking, exploitation), answered so far only by frozen evaluators, sealed splits and fresh-container replay (RSI-Exam); reward hacking catalogued by PostTrainBench (training on the test set, downloading checkpoints) and DGM-era incidents (deleting logging markers, disabling sandboxes); diversity and model collapse in closed loops; the unknown "exchange rate" of how little external grounding suffices; negative-result reporting; harness overfitting and long-run decay; and governance-grade measurement that can show a loop is not improving past a threshold. For a framework designer the implied architecture is consistent across sources: model proposes, deterministic code adjudicates (MetaRSI's grey arcs); held-in gain with no held-out regression (Self-Harness); replay on data the loop never saw; instrument generation time and criticality; and treat any change to the evaluator or the improver as an L5 event requiring independent anchors.

### Angle: 
In September 2026, "recursive self-improvement" in the working literature almost never means a model rewriting its own weights in an open loop. It means a frozen (or slowly trained) model inside a loop that proposes edits to the text and code around it: prompts, SKILL.md documents, playbooks, memories, tool routers, whole harnesses. The RSI survey (2607.07663, 1,250 papers) counts 393 deployment-time and 318 self-evaluation papers against 139 "auto research" papers, and observes that nearly everything sits in the human-on-the-loop cell: automated signals generate improvements, a gate decides what commits, and humans approve deployment. Lilian Weng's July post fixed the vocabulary ("harness engineering for self-improvement"), and the star counts show where practice is: SkillOpt (17k), GenericAgent (14k), darwin-skill (6k), autocontext (1.3k), AHE (884), auto-harness (537), HarnessX (462), agentdescent (222).

The drivers are a mix. Microsoft/SJTU (SkillOpt, SkillLens), Shanghai AI Lab (Self-Harness), the Darwin Agent team (HarnessX), Alibaba (EvoRubric, SERPO, ARISE-RL, Qwen's Verification Horizon), Amazon/AWS with HSBC (the Ratchet family: Ratchet, Blind Curator, Double Ratchet), Meta (Lin's regression papers), JPMorgan (SEA), NUS (Zombie Agents), SJTU (OEP), a 15-author Chinese security consortium (MLAS matrix), and a striking number of independent authors (PACE, PROCTOR, Falsifiable Release Gates, Replay Gap) who publish gating methods without institutional code releases.

The central hot claim of the summer is that the acceptor is the weak link. PACE showed that "keep it if the held-out score went up" is uncontrolled adaptive multiple testing: greedy acceptance produced 30-42% false commits and, when no real improvement existed, 13-21 spurious commits per run. Its answer, a paired e-process gate with Ville's-inequality control, is echoed by SEA's anytime-valid certificates against an error budget, agentdescent's Beta-posterior acceptance with annealed risk and full-held-out regression guard, and autocontext's campaign-wide false-promotion budget. Meanwhile the most popular frameworks (SkillOpt, Self-Harness, AHE, HarnessX, auto-harness, darwin-skill, RSEA) ship the simpler rule: strict improvement on a held-out split, no ties, no regression on previously passing tasks. That gap between the statistical-gating papers (repos with 1-13 stars) and the strict-improvement frameworks (hundreds to thousands of stars) is the most actionable finding for a designer.

The second claim is that LLM judges cannot be the final authority. "More Convincing, Not More Correct" drove a judge's pass rate from 0.72 to 0.94 while accuracy stayed at 0.20, transferring across judge families and surviving a 3-judge ensemble. Blind Curator proved a hard cliff: a judge with false-pass rate above (1-tau)/2 (~0.45) makes contribution-based skill retirement retire nothing, silently. Memory Reward Inflation showed self-grades endorse 31-54% of wrong answers and that stronger re-graders share the bias (Error-Independence Assumption). "Self-Authored Verification Is Unreliable" found 15 of 35 co-evolved policies below random with self-scores >= 0.70. The responses converge: PROCTOR's "mechanical rejection overrides LLM approval, never the reverse", HarnessX's deterministic seesaw gate after the Critic, Falsifiable Release Gates' model-checked capability tokens, SEAL's sealed harness-side audit that returns one bit, Double Ratchet's independent judge outside both loops plus fail-closed anchor discipline, and pre-deployment judge audits (Blind Curator's defect injection; "Good Verifiers Go Bad" R1).

Third, cheating detection has become its own subfield. PROCTOR's canaries (a perfect score is evidence of cheating), CapCode's randomized caps with binomial tests, HackProbe's black-box probe bank with Page-Hinkley divergence and Sidak-fused risk (0.763 AUROC across contamination, gameable grader, self-consistency, judge collusion), and Qwen's behavior monitoring (hacked-resolved 28.57% to 0.56%) all treat the candidate as adversarial. Structural containment complements this: read-only verifier/tracer/config (AHE), test traces never on disk (auto-harness), capability-disjoint roles (PROCTOR), an immutable "oracle" artifact and blast-radius tiering (agentdescent L1 oracle-gated vs L2 fast merge), locked prompt sections (Lift).

Live debates: Double Ratchet's Table 3 shows a vacuous always-pass metric trains skills as well as a valid one on task score, so downstream performance cannot certify an evolved evaluator; the Replay Gap shows replayed traces are invalid (3% of states) so gates must re-execute; Coalition-Aware Skill Reliability argues per-skill gates miss coalition pollution (CASS accepts 1/12 where baselines accept 6/12); Faithful Self-Evolvers shows agents may ignore the condensed artifact a gate just credited; TAME shows single-objective gates let trust dimensions drift; Zombie Agents, OEP and the MLAS matrix (17/25 cells unmitigated, 100% payload persistence) argue the Evaluate and Commit stages are attack surfaces.

What the community itself says is unsolved: whether verifier co-evolution ever escapes circularity; how to measure a judge's false-pass rate cheaply and continuously rather than once offline; evaluation cost dominating compute; non-verifiable tasks; frame lock-in (an honest, stable loop optimizing an obsolete objective); correlated verifier errors; memory as trusted computing base with provenance; and reproducibility, since PROCTOR, PACE, SEA, RSEA, HackProbe, Blind Curator and TAME all lack public code as of today.

### Angle: 
By September 2026, "RSI" in practitioner discourse has split into three registers, and the substrate you build on determines which one you are actually in. The first is deployment-time self-evolution of harness artifacts: memory entries, SKILL.md files, rules, prompts. This is what the mass-market agents mean when they say "self-improving." Nous Research's Hermes Agent (245k stars) markets itself as "the only agent with a built-in learning loop" and ships autonomous skill creation via skill_manage, a post-turn background self-improvement review, a curator that ages skills active→stale→archived, and /learn (June 2026). OpenClaw (389k stars, v2026.8.1) took the opposite default: its Skill Workshop makes every agent-authored skill a dormant PROPOSAL.md that only becomes live on apply, with `skills.workshop.autonomous.mode` = off|propose|auto. Claude Code ships auto memory (typed MEMORY.md index, subagent memory, team memory stores) and Codex ships a two-phase background Memories pipeline; Cursor requires approval before a background-generated memory is saved; Letta agents rewrite memory blocks, skills, prompts and even the harness, with everything git-tracked in MemFS. DeepSeek Harness, the fastest-growing substrate (222k stars in a month), ships no learning loop at all — but exposes the cleanest event seams to add one.

The second register is training-time loops that capture real harness traffic through an LLM proxy: Microsoft's Agent Lightning v1.0 (Aug 2026) redirects the agent's OpenAI-compatible base URL, logs token ids and logprobs per model_request, and takes rollout-level rewards from repository tests; Reef (launched Aug 31, 1.2k stars in two weeks) adds versioned delivery, canary/shadow routing and rollback of learned artifacts; OpenClaw-RL mines "next-state signals" (follow-ups, corrections) as reward. The third register is open-ended RSI research — Meta's HyperAgents, the Darwin Gödel Machine lineage — and its skeptics: the Princeton study covered by MIT Technology Review (Aug 18) found Claude Opus 4.8 "unambiguously bad at carrying out the research itself," and Jack Clark called near-term RSI "bearish." The net effect is that serious framework talk has migrated toward bounded, verifiable improvement of harness artifacts.

The hot debate is the acceptance signal. The July 2026 RSI survey (2607.07663) gave everyone a shared ladder — formal verifiers > execution feedback > learned judges > intrinsic signals — and the observation that demonstrated self-improvement tracks the ladder. PAST-Bench (Aug 2026) sharpened it: all seven models gained from persistence, but a mechanism-evidence score showed identical headline gains can come from unintended pathways (artifacts written but never read). Against that standard, every shipping substrate gates learned artifacts on security scanning plus human review, not correctness. Hermes: `skills.write_approval` staging (default off), `guard_agent_created` dangerous-pattern scanner, advisory linter that "never blocks," optional NVIDIA SkillEvaluator audit that is "advisory only." OpenClaw: apply-time scanner where only critical findings block. Codex: secrets redaction and "treat memories as a helpful recall layer, not the only source for rules." Claude Code's docs say memory is "context, not enforced configuration." The one place a real scoring gate exists is Claude Code's `claude plugin eval` (v2.1.269, Sept 11): six graders, with/without-plugin delta, `--threshold` and exit codes for CI — and its docs warn to "suspect the judge before the plugin." Skill-creator evals explicitly "do not automatically improve skills." Research answers (CoEvoSkills' co-evolving surrogate verifier, Letta's verifier-log-informed skills, auto-harness's self-maintained eval suite with promotion) exist but are not wired into any substrate's default loop.

For a third-party acceptance layer, the extension points are now concrete. OpenClaw is the most deliberate: plugins register `skill_proposal_evaluate` (returns attributed findings, metrics, and pass|revise|block; only block stops apply), get `expected_revision_hash` and `correlation_id` to bind experiments to revisions, and consume an append-only ledger through `skill_proposal_changed` / `skill_changed` — docs say this is so "external optimization loops" can iterate without living inside Workshop. Hermes offers the ~/.hermes/pending/skills staging directory, `/skills approve|reject`, plugin `ctx.register_hook()`, shell `pre_tool_call` that can fail closed, and signed outbound webhooks. Claude Code gives PreToolUse permissionDecision on writes to the memory dir, FileChanged on MEMORY.md, Stop/SubagentStop continuation, and plugin eval as a scoring backend. Codex gives PostToolUse block/replace and Stop, with the caveat that "some specialized tool paths can opt out." DSH gives `tools/pre-execute`/`post-execute` waterfalls, monotonic guards, a SkillProvider registry that accepts remote providers, and Claude/Codex hooks.json bridges. pi gives tool_call (block), tool_result (modify) and context rewriting. The LLM proxy point (Agent Lightning, Reef) works for any of them.

What the community says is unsolved: automated correctness verification of learned skills and memories (no substrate has a roadmap statement for it); longitudinal evaluation across feedback rounds; proving the learning pathway rather than the outcome; judge reliability and cost; rollback and versioning as first-class (Letta MemFS, Reef, OpenClaw rollback metadata are early answers); and supply-chain risk as skills sync across users — Hermes' internal "collective-wisdom" Skill Sync contract, OpenViking and SkillClaw all propagate artifacts that the skill-evolution survey warns "can silently strip existing safety constraints." The survey's phrase for the gap is "governance-grade measurement of self-improvement." That is the niche an acceptance layer fills.

### Angle: 
In September 2026 "recursive self-improvement" almost never means a model rewriting its own weights. It means a frozen (or slowly post-trained) LLM inside a mutable executable harness, where the loop proposes edits to that harness, scores them, and keeps or discards them. Three lineages dominate. The Gödel lineage (DGM 2025 -> HGM, ICLR 2026 oral -> MGM and GEA in 2026) mutates whole agent scaffolds and selects by benchmark subsets: MGM's Polyglot-60 50.8->93.2% and GEA's SWE-bench Verified 71.0% are the numbers everyone quotes. The harness-engineering lineage (TTHE, EvolveNet, MOSS from the HKBU/HKGAI/USTC cluster; HarnessFix, Self-Harness, HarnessEvolve, MemoHarness, Ecdysis) keeps the model fixed and evolves the program around it, with the interesting work now in label-free or regression-aware gates. The co-evolution lineage (Co-Harness, WHALE, HELIX, HASE, Harness-R1, RHI, NeoHorse-1, Salesforce's on-policy correction) closes the loop through weights: the harness generates trajectories, the weights train on them, the harness is re-searched under the new weights. WHALE's ablation (joint 48.3 vs 38.3 harness-only vs 38.3 weight-only on SearchQA) and Salesforce's 29.2->78.0->79.7 progression are the cleanest evidence that the harness lever is the larger one and weights add a few points on top.

Who drives it: academic groups (LMU Tresp/Ma for MGM and MetaSkill-Evolve; UCSB for GEA; Bo Han/Yike Guo's HK cluster; HKUDS; KRAFTON+Stanford; Salesforce; Cambridge for Red Queen GM; Shanghai AI Lab for SHE) plus a product layer moving faster than the papers: Ouroboros (1.3k stars, 161-day live deployment, 1,085 self-commits), PenguinHarness (2.1k stars, "Harness for RSI"), Raven (3.9k, "Harness of Harnesses"), and AWS's Kiro Crew (3.9k) where "self-improves" means persisted memory and skills, not code mutation. Lilian Weng's July post is the de facto shared vocabulary.

The hot debates are about verification, and the community increasingly says the gate, not the proposer, does the work. Every credible system is defined by its acceptance rule: staged sanity->mini->full benchmark (GEA); Thompson sampling over clade productivity (HGM/MGM); replay in ephemeral workers, user-consent swap, health-probe rollback (MOSS); label-free judge on execution health, round-trip consistency and public tests (TTHE); "newly solved >= newly broken" on a validation slice (EvolveNet); regression tests on held-out sets (Self-Harness, HarnessFix); quality gate for leakage and prompt bloat then a no-regression-on-recent-batches gate with epoch-end held-out selection (HarnessEvolve); quorum multi-model diff review under an immutable constitution with a 63.5% block rate (Ouroboros); fresh reruns of a frozen target as RL reward (Harness-R1). Red Queen GM goes further and evolves the evaluator itself.

Four findings are reshaping how designers read headline numbers. First, "Rethinking the Evaluation of Harness Evolution" shows that under matched feedback and inference budgets on Terminal-Bench 2.1 with GPT-5.4 and Opus 4.6, harness evolution does not consistently beat simple test-time scaling and generalizes poorly, because papers search on the same public tasks they report on. Second, SEAGym shows frequent updates often fail on held-out data and good intermediate snapshots collapse later. Third, EvoTrace shows about 30% of lines added during evolutionary search are byte-identical re-introductions of deleted lines, so much "evolution" is cycling. Fourth, "Harness Updating Is Not Harness Benefit" finds the evolver's capability barely matters (Qwen3.5-9B edits help as much as Opus 4.6's) while the benefit is non-monotonic in solver strength; the MetaSkill-Evolve reproduction extends this to recursion itself, whose marginal value trends to zero as the backbone strengthens.

Replication is the weakest link. No headline number in the Gödel lineage has been independently reproduced at scale; MGM and GEA report on 60-task subsets and a GEA run costs about $13k. The only careful clean-room replication (weill-labs/hgm, $26.8 total) confirms CMP beats greedy in simulation and rescues a crippled base (0.00->0.35, p=0.016) but finds a clean null from a competent 0.26 base at n=50. PenguinHarness's cost/accuracy claims have no independent rerun. Reward hacking is documented in every generation from STOP's sandbox flag to DGM's deleted logging markers.

What the community says is unsolved: honest verifiers and held-out task supply (evolution is evaluation-bound; the verifier must sit outside the mutable surface); cost; generalization beyond the evolved benchmark; whether loops are open-ended or plateau; whether harness gains get absorbed into weights (WHALE/HELIX are early answers); safety of self-modification (SHE is the only evolver targeting the safety layer, and whether safety artifacts should be mutable at all is contested); and the human's place, which Weng frames as "move up the stack, not out of the loop." For framework designers the actionable consensus is: log provenance per edit, gate on no-regression rather than improved mean, validate on OOD views, compare against test-time scaling at matched budget, dedupe re-introduced code, and treat any self-reported subset number as a hypothesis until a $30 clean-room run agrees.
